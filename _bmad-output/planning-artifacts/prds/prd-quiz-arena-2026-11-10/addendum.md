## UX Tone — Battle Room

*For the UX designer (Sally) when designing the Battle Room flow.*

Battle Room's closest cultural analogue is the WhatsApp batch battle that Kerala coaching centers already run. Teachers drop a question in a group chat; students race to reply. The feel is instant, informal, and social — not a formal exam lobby.

This has UX implications:
- Joining a room via code should feel like joining a WhatsApp group, not checking in to a test hall. Minimal friction, no confirmation ceremonies.
- The waiting lobby (players joined but match not started) should feel alive — show who's joined, maybe a countdown the host controls.
- The live scoreboard during the match is the social heartbeat. Players aren't just competing; they're watching each other. Make movement feel exciting, not clinical.
- Post-match screen is a moment of banter — scores, right/wrong, maybe a "GG" mechanism. Not a silent grade sheet.

The product wins if Battle Room feels like it was designed for the way Kerala students already study together, not like a formal test that happens to allow multiple players.
