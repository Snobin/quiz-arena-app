Technique 01 · Job to Be Done

What are they really hiring this for?

Forget features. When an aspirant opens a quiz app at 10 pm after a long day, what is the actual job?

The real job discovered

Feel like making real progress — not just grinding.

Not "study for PSC." Not "complete a mock test." The underlying anxiety is: I don't know if I'm ready. Aspirants need a confidence mirror — proof they could pass if the exam was tomorrow.

↑ User
Feel real progress, not grind
↑ Partner
"Am I ready?" is the anxiety. Confidence mirror, not content volume.
↑ Partner
Social sting of losing a live battle = stronger memory hook than any flashcard
↑ User
Learning happens effortlessly through battling — it's the exhaust, not the engine
Key positioning unlock

Battle-only. No classes. No courses.

PSC Challenger puts battle on top of a course platform. Prepscale is course-first, battle as an afterthought. Minnal inverts this entirely: the battle is the product. Learning is what leaks out.

Technique 02 · Game Modes

Three modes. Ship it. Grow it later.

Start focused. The room mechanic is the differentiator — no competitor has it in this form.

⚔️

Quick Match

1v1 matchmaking. Bot fills the seat if no human appears in ~8 seconds. Never a cold lobby.

🎯

Topic Duel

1v1, topic-locked. Challenge someone to Kerala History specifically. Settle the score.

🏟️

Battle Room

2–N players. Free-for-all. Same questions, live scoreboard ticking as answers land. Pure pressure.

The room insight

Live scoreboard changes everything.

In a 5-player room you watch 4 people simultaneously. If you're 3rd after question 6, you know exactly who to overtake. That psychological pressure is what turns a practice session into a habit. It's the same mechanic that makes watching live cricket scores compelling even from a distance.

Technique 04 · What If Scenarios

How it earns.

Free for users. Coaching centers are the revenue layer — eventually.

The B2B2C model

Coaching centers pay. Students follow.

Kerala coaching centers already run informal batch battles on WhatsApp — "answer this question, first correct reply wins." Minnal doesn't need to teach a new behaviour. It formalizes what centers already want to do, adds tracking and analytics, and charges the center — not the student.

Free tier
Unlimited battles for all players forever
↑ Partner
Teacher dashboard: see the whole batch's weak topics in one screen
↑ Partner
Scheduled batch rooms, recurring weekly tests
Revenue
₹500–2000/month per coaching center — cheaper than printing mock tests
Synthesis · The Connections

What the session revealed.

These aren't just individual ideas — they lock together.

  • 1
    Battle = disguised spaced repetition. Live scoreboard + competitive pressure = the core learning mechanism. No explicit study UX needed. The sting of losing in front of batchmates is a stronger memory hook than any flashcard app.
  • 2
    Coaching centers are the distribution channel. Sign up a center, their students follow — no paid B2C acquisition. The teacher dashboard turns teachers into evangelists.
  • 3
    WhatsApp-culture fit. The free-for-all room mechanic maps directly onto how Kerala coaching batches already coordinate informally. Lowest friction adoption path in the market.
  • 4
    General quiz + Kerala-first = natural expansion runway. PSC is the launch wedge, but Minnal extends to school competitions, college quiz fests, and general Kerala trivia culture without a rebrand. No competitor owns that space.
  • 5
    The name does double duty. Minnal = lightning = speed, the core mechanic. And it's inherently Kerala. No Bangalore startup can claim it.
